Where things stand

The shape of the week

Revenue reached a record and the operational back end did not keep pace. Refunds for the week to 1 September ran $54k on Maggie, $9k on Mary, $3k on Elsie and $589 on Lyn. By 3 September that was running at roughly $10k a day on Maggie alone against about $195k of daily revenue.

We just hit 200k days. That was not the goal. But we did. Reber — Daily Recap, 2 Sep

Customer support genuinely improved: tickets fell 4% to 9,885 and resolutions rose 5%, with 21 tasks completed against 8 pending and 2 overdue. But the underlying product and supply problems are unchanged, and they are now scaling with the revenue.

8% to 10% refund is very high, and we cannot continue with the business for a long time in reality if we have this high amount of refund. Jerone — Founders Meeting, 1 Sep

You stopped placing forecast orders two weeks earlier because 24,000 units were already in production and a new sample was coming. Gary then told the founders you weren't ordering enough. The support AI hit its limit and nobody raised it for two days. Agents answered manually, response times slipped, and customers waiting on replacements and refunds gave up and asked for their money back. Ahmed's read is that the outage is the symptom and the escalation failure is the finding.

None of the agents escalated to Sara or Rose or even you. You need to figure out why that happened. Ahmed — Founders Meeting, 1 Sep

Decisions made

  • Bean paste production stopped. It is about 2% of sales. Focus on nude, black, white and yellow.
  • Split shipments approved for orders containing backordered colours — ship what is available now.
  • Reward over punishment for agent performance. Ahmed wants a visible bonus for a strong agent shared in the channel.
  • Hire in a block of ten, train them together on one Monday with Sarah reviewing live tickets on a group call, then keep the best.
  • Branding goes on the garment, not just the packaging, from the next production run.

  • A fabric and sizing consultant will be hired. Candidates seen: Yefim (20+ years, Victoria's Secret, Target, Adore Me), Rosee Aranda, plus Heidi and Victoria. One offers a two-week China project for $7k.

Still unresolved

Open risks, most consequential first

The root cause is pattern grading, not the size chart

Rosee Aranda's assessment on 4 September is that plus sizes run small because the grading was applied wrong around L and XL. Fixing it needs three grading tiers and the factory's front and back patterns. That reframes weeks of size-chart argument as a pattern problem — and it cannot be fixed by editing a chart on Shopify.

The fabric change may undercut the product

The proposal is 68% nylon / 32% spandex with a vertical cut. More spandex plus vertical cut means more stretch, and the product is advertised as shapewear that compresses. You raised this on 3 September and it was deferred to the consultant rather than answered.

Nobody has established real factory capacity

Three different figures in three weeks, none verified. The 15,000 working number was chosen defensively, not confirmed. Every forecast rests on it.

A possible copycat on Amazon may trace back to Mega Fulfill

Reber's read is that the Amazon copycat could be produced by a factory belonging to a relative of the Mega Fulfill principals. Agreed worth checking in person. No owner assigned.

Email and SMS are actively creating tickets

Out-of-stock emails went to customers still waiting on orders. The Zobi SMS app is sending without customer details. Both are generating the complaints that support then absorbs.
