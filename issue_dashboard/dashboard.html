Source and completeness. Every figure is the complete server-side count for the selected period from four Commslayer accounts — Simply Elsie (7356), Maggie’s Tanks (6576), Mary’s Tanks (6377) and Lyn’s Tanks (6527). Nothing here is a sample or an extrapolation.
  • Why aggregates, not ticket rows. The conversation list caps at 500 records per account and reaches back only 30 days, so it cannot cover 1 July onward for 74,429 tickets. Counts come from the reporting endpoints, which aggregate the whole population server-side.
  • Resolution. Ticket volume is exact per day. Issue and reason splits are exact per week, so the date filter snaps outward to whole weeks and the effective range is always shown. Nothing is interpolated between weeks.
  • Stores are covered to different dates. Simply Elsie, Mary’s Tanks and Lyn’s Tanks run to 25 August; Maggie’s Tanks runs to 1 September. Commslayer’s connection is now pinned to one account at a time and no longer exposes account switching, so only Maggie’s could be refreshed past 25 August. Any range ending after 25 August therefore shows Incomplete Data, names the stores that fall short, and prints — rather than 0 in their columns — a store that was not retrieved never reads as a store with no tickets. Uncovered stores are excluded from the KPIs, totals and chart for that range rather than being counted as zero. For a like-for-like four-store comparison, keep the end date at 25 August.
  • Today is partial. Maggie’s window runs to 1 September, which was still in progress when this was built (about 09:00 UTC). The final day — and so the final week bucket — is short, so read the last point as incomplete rather than as a fall. The trend reading compares per-day rates for exactly this reason.
  • Tickets vs issues. One ticket can carry more than one issue. A reason such as “item arrived damaged and has a sizing issue” is counted as 1 ticket and 2 issues (Product Quality → Damaged, Sizing). 26 of the source reasons are multi-issue, which is why total issues exceeds total tickets.
  • Issue reasons. Reasons are the helpdesk’s own classification of each ticket, so they are complete rather than inferred. The source does not record the direction of a fit failure, so “too small” versus “too big” is not separable from it — sizing reasons name what the source does state. Anything unclassified is shown as Other / Needs review.
  • Known variances, verified. Weekly counts were reconciled against an independently fetched full-period total for all four accounts: 1 of 365 reason nodes differed, by 1 ticket in 60,485 (0.002%), from a ticket being reclassified mid-fetch. Mary’s Tanks reports in UTC+10, so its daily series covers 16,241 of its 16,304 tickets (0.4%) — the remainder falls outside the UTC day buckets. Both are shown rather than smoothed.
  • Colour. Colour is counted only where a reason explicitly names it. Maggie’s Tanks added “order exchange and colour correction” during August, which is why the colour row is no longer near-zero — it still reflects what the helpdesk records, not the true rate of colour complaints.
  • Open and resolved. Resolved counts closures recorded inside the range, which can include tickets raised before it, so a resolution rate above 100% means the backlog shrank rather than that every ticket was answered. Open is created minus closures; where that is negative the card shows 0 and reports the reduction instead. The reporting API also exposes a created-cohort closure total (72,306 across the period) measured differently from the daily closure series (76,522); the daily series is used throughout because it is the one that filters by date, and mixing the two would break the single-dataset rule.
  • Ticket total. Totals are the sum of the daily series over whatever stores cover the selected range. To 25 August across all four that is 65,409; the whole dataset, with Maggie’s alone running on to 1 September, is 74,366. The accounts’ own period totals add to 74,429; the 63-ticket difference is Mary’s UTC+10 boundary described above. The lower, internally consistent figure is used everywhere so the KPIs, table, breakdown and chart cannot disagree.
