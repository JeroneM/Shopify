Source and completeness. Every figure is the complete server-side count for the selected period from four Commslayer accounts — Simply Elsie (7356), Maggie’s Tanks (6576), Mary’s Tanks (6377) and Lyn’s Tanks (6527). Nothing here is a sample or an extrapolation.
  • Why aggregates, not ticket rows. The conversation list caps at 500 records per account and reaches back only 30 days, so it cannot cover 1 July onward for 156,252 tickets. Counts come from the reporting endpoints, which aggregate the whole population server-side.
  • Resolution. Ticket volume is exact per day. Issue and reason splits are exact per week, so the date filter snaps outward to whole weeks and the effective range is always shown. Nothing is interpolated between weeks.
  • All four stores are current. Each store has its own Commslayer connection, so all four were fetched over the same fourteen weekly windows and every one covers 1 July – 4 October. If a store ever falls behind the dashboard prints — rather than 0 in its columns and excludes it from the KPIs for that range.
  • No partial days. The window ends 4 October, the last fully-elapsed day, so every day in the dataset is complete.
  • History is not frozen — everything was re-fetched. Past weeks keep moving, so each refresh re-fetches them rather than carrying figures forward. This build found three kinds of movement against the 29 September capture: 1,944 of Maggie’s closures were reversed (tickets resolved between 9 and 28 September that a customer reply has since reopened, −260 on 28 September alone); one Maggie’s ticket created 5 September no longer exists; and one of Mary’s 27 September closures was undone. Figures for ranges you looked at before will have shifted accordingly.
  • Tickets vs issues. One ticket can carry more than one issue. A reason such as “item arrived damaged and has a sizing issue” is counted as 1 ticket and 2 issues (Product Quality → Damaged, Sizing). 29 of the source reasons are multi-issue, which is why total issues exceeds total classified tickets.
  • Issue reasons. Reasons are the helpdesk’s own classification of each ticket, so they are complete rather than inferred. The source does not record the direction of a fit failure, so “too small” versus “too big” is not separable from it — sizing reasons name what the source does state. Anything unclassified is shown as Other / Needs review.
  • Classification coverage. 131,455 of the 156,252 tickets (84.1%) carry a contact reason; the rest are unclassified at source. The issue table, breakdown and issue KPIs count the classified population, while Total Tickets counts every ticket — so issue counts will not add up to the ticket total, by design.
  • Verification. Each store’s fourteen weekly windows were summed and checked node by node against an independently fetched total for the whole period. All four reconcile exactly: 401 reason nodes, 0 differences, and each store’s daily series sums to its own reported period total to the ticket. The one mismatch this build threw up — a single Maggie’s edit order ticket — was traced to the week of 2 September and corrected rather than absorbed.
  • Colour. Colour is counted only where a reason explicitly names it. Maggie’s Tanks added “order exchange and colour correction” during August, which is why the colour row is no longer near-zero — it still reflects what the helpdesk records, not the true rate of colour complaints.
  • Open and resolved. Resolved counts closures recorded inside the range, which can include tickets raised before it, so a resolution rate above 100% means the backlog shrank rather than that every ticket was answered. Open is created minus closures; where that is negative the card shows 0 and reports the reduction instead. The reporting API also exposes a created-cohort closure total (139,965 across the period) measured differently from the daily closure series (185,812); the daily series is used throughout because it is the one that filters by date, and mixing the two would break the single-dataset rule.
  • Ticket total. Totals are the sum of the daily series over the selected range. Across all four stores for the whole period that is 156,252, which equals the accounts’ own period totals exactly (3,260 + 125,089 + 24,822 + 3,081). The same series feeds the KPIs, table, breakdown and chart, so they cannot disagree.
